# Landing page v3: white, light blue and geometric motion

The public landing page was redesigned in white and light blue, with Sora headings, DM Sans text, a once-per-tab opening, a 3D ring and one coordinated set of geometric section transitions. Its content and rules are unchanged: EduSuite stays the system of record, AI output stays a draft until people approve it, Pulse does not answer assessments, EduPulse shows scores but not official grades, the CIT is the current scope, and sign-in still uses administrator-created accounts (email and password, Google for the system admin). Local implementation and validation; pushed to the `landing-v3-light` branch, not deployed.

## Features

- **Palette and type:** `#FFFFFF`, `#F4F9FF`, `#DBEAFE`, `#2563EB`, `#0F172A`, `#475569` and `#E2E8F0` as `--lp-*` tokens scoped to `.landing-page`; Sora 700 to 800 for the name and headings, DM Sans for text. Fraunces was removed from `index.html`. The landing page keeps one light palette in both themes; signed-in screens are unchanged.
- **Opening:** on the first visit to `/` in a tab, the logo and name assemble on a light field with geometric planes and 3D facets (about 1.3 s); scrolling about one screen opens the planes like an aperture onto the hero, and scrolling back closes it. **Skip intro** or keyboard focus removes it. A `sessionStorage` flag (`edupulse-intro`), reloads, Back and Forward, section links (`#/?section=`), later returns and reduced motion skip it.
- **3D scene:** one three.js canvas in its own lazily loaded chunk (`src/components/landing/scene/landingScene.js`): one renderer, one material program compiled before the first frame, one camera controller (entrance push-in, aperture pull-back, clamped pointer parallax for a mouse), rendering only on screen in a visible tab, disposed on unmount. Without WebGL, with reduced motion or on failure, an SVG ring stands in.
- **Sections:** *Why EduPulse* is five flip cards with a **Today** | **With EduPulse** switch (it was a table); Features pins on a desktop, where one value drives the step number, heading, screen, caption and step buttons; the phase connector draws with scrolling; role tabs have a moving marker, directional masks and a stable height; the Pulse example opens in a circle (its refusal demo is unchanged); research figures are not animated as counters; arcs draw around the CIT seal; the sign-in photograph opens from a rectangle while the form is never masked or moved.
- **Inside Pulse (new section, after Pulse):** Pulse's nine named agents from `src/lib/rag/agents.ts` (Guardian, Planner, Researcher, Librarian, Ranker, Comparator, Writer, Verifier, Corrector) as concentric rings of type around a blue core, neighbouring rings turning in opposite directions. With motion the section pins: scrolling travels inward one ring per agent while a card names it, then the core opens into white and hands over to the agent workspace. Without motion it is a still picture and a numbered list; the list always carries the agents for screen readers. One SVG and one scroll-linked timeline; the browser spreads each ring's letters (`textLength`).
- **Hero row:** the product screen and the CIT photograph share one row (two thirds and one third). At that split the photograph's own 9:11 shape fills the height the screen sets, so both end on the same line at every width; the ring sits on the photograph's corner. Before, the photograph stood in a narrow column under the ring and left an empty band beside it.
- **Fewer words:** the copy of *Why EduPulse*, Features, How it works, Roles, Pulse, *Why it matters* and Colleges was cut to one idea per line; the facts and the boundaries (EduSuite as the record, people approve, no assessment answers, scores not grades) are unchanged, and the FAQ keeps the full answers.
- **Agent portraits:** the nine agents share one look (`agentLook.js`, `AgentFace.jsx`): a white robot with a dark face screen and glowing eyes, its ears, antenna and chest panel in the agent's colour, holding up its prop. In the scope, the agent being named waves from a lens at the centre with its own gesture; while the core opens, all nine wave together and fade with the light. The still list shows each agent's portrait.
- **Agent workspace (new section after the scope):** a cosy 3D studio, the second three.js scene in its own chunk, grown from the in-app office (`components/pulse/office`). The robots work at their stations; tapping one (or its button) makes it stand, turn to the camera, wave and answer; three scripted requests fly between the agents they need as paper planes while the camera follows, and a declined one bounces off the Guardian. Every line is scripted from what the agents do in `src/lib/rag/agents.ts` and the section says *A scripted example, not live AI*. **Pause motion** stops its idle motion (WCAG 2.2.2); the buttons and a polite live conversation list work by keyboard and screen reader; without WebGL a grid of the agents' icons stands in; with reduced motion every change jumps.
- **Photographs (WCAG 2.2.2):** a persistent **Pause photo movement** control, plus hover, keyboard focus and tap. Testing found that keyboard focus right after a click on that control was ignored for 1.5 s; the pointer-focus window now closes at the click.
- **Navigation:** section links glide with GSAP's ScrollToPlugin and finish under the bar; scrolling yourself stops the glide; scroll anchoring is off on the landing page. Privacy opens with a rectangular reveal and Terms with a diagonal one (CSS, 320 to 360 ms); Back restores the landing scroll position.

## Dependencies and build

- No new dependencies. `three` (already a dependency) now loads only in the two scene chunks: the opening (5.67 kB) and the agent workspace (39.90 kB, with three.js's RoomEnvironment, RoundedBoxGeometry and BufferGeometryUtils), plus three.js itself (560.35 kB, shared). Both load lazily: the opening when the page is idle, the workspace when its section comes near. The landing page no longer imports `lenis`; the package stays in `package.json`.
- Animations use GSAP 3.15 (ScrollTrigger, SplitText, ScrollToPlugin) inside `gsap.matchMedia`, so reduced motion reverts them; simple states use CSS.

## Known limitations

- The test machine had little free memory, so browser tests ran with one worker and generous timeouts.
- The 3D scene is checked for start-up cost, disposal and the WebGL fallback, not across real GPUs and phones.

## Combined release check, 30 October 2026

The combined release keeps all of these frontend changes: the public scripted Agent workspace (a lazily loaded robot studio, per-agent buttons, sample hand-offs, a conversation list, reduced-motion support and a flat WebGL fallback, labelled as a scripted example, not live AI) and the Pulse office that now shows the same studio. It adds the backend content harness and the bounded API request intake. Final combined validation is recorded in [the integration release](../2026-10-30-integrated-release/validation/results.md).
